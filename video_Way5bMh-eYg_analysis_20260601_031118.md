**Trowel Technique and Thin-Set Application**
*   **Keying In:** Always start by using the flat side of the trowel to "key in" or burn a coat of mortar into the substrate to establish a strong mechanical bond.
*   **Straight Lines:** Comb the mortar in straight lines, with all ridges running in the same direction. 
*   **Avoid Swirls:** Never swirl the mortar. Swirling traps air, creates voids, and leads to weak spots, cracked tiles, and bond failure. Swirling adhesive is only appropriate for resilient flooring, not tile.
*   **No Spot Bonding:** Spot bonding (applying dabs of mortar to the corners and center) is highly discouraged as it leaves massive voids and provides inadequate support.
*   **Direction for Rectangular Tile:** When installing rectangular tiles, comb the ridges across the short side of the tile. This provides the shortest path for air to escape when the tile is embedded.
*   **Trowel Selection:** Use a smaller trowel for smaller tiles and a larger trowel with deeper notches for larger tiles to ensure enough mortar is present to achieve coverage.

**Coverage Requirements**
*   **Standard Interior:** ANSI A108.5 requires a minimum of **80%** mortar coverage for standard interior applications.
*   **Wet/Exterior/Stone:** A minimum of **95%** mortar coverage is required for wet environments, exterior applications, and all natural stone installations.

**Back-Buttering**
*   Back-buttering is recommended for most tiles, especially large format tiles. This involves using the flat side of the trowel to apply a thin, even coat of mortar to the back of the tile before setting it. This helps achieve a strong bond and fills voids caused by the natural warpage or "doming" that occurs during the manufacturing of large tiles.

**Tile Placement**
*   **Embedding:** Place the tile and move it back and forth *across* (perpendicular to) the trowel ridges to collapse the ridges and push the air out. 
*   **Do Not Twist:** Avoid twisting the tile, as this does not effectively collapse the ridges.
*   **Check Coverage:** Periodically lift a newly set tile to inspect the back and ensure the required coverage is being achieved. 

**Quality Standards and Preparation**
*   The video references the **ANSI A108.5** standard and the **TCNA (Tile Council of North America) Handbook**.
*   **Surface Preparation:** Proper surface preparation is required. 
*   **Floor Flatness:** For standard tile, variations in floor flatness should not exceed 1/4" in 10 feet.
*   **Large Format Tile Flatness:** For large format tile (defined as any tile with a side longer than 15"), the substrate must be even flatter: variations should not exceed 1/8" in 10 feet, or 1/16" in 24 inches.
